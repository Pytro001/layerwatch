LayerWatch: evaluation report

Layer by layer vision QA for laser powder bed fusion, built on OpenCV 5.0.0. Generated by make report from docs/eval/*.json; every number below is read from those files.

1. What is measured

  • Synthetic hold-out: 400 layers from 10 simulated builds (new camera, lamp, powder and defect seeds) generated after all thresholds were frozen. None of these images was used for training, validation or tuning. A second synthetic split ("dev test", 400 layers) was looked at while tuning thresholds; its numbers are in docs/eval/synthetic_test.json and are not the headline.
  • Real images: 250 labelled powder bed frames of an EOS M290 (Aalto University dataset, CC-BY 4.0, doi:10.5281/zenodo.14996805), zero shot: recoat checks only, thresholds unchanged, no DNN.
  • Closed loop demo build: 480 layers through the real handler with scripted events (section 6).

Scoring is per layer: a defect type counts as found on a layer if the detector reports at least one region of that type. Frames with strong glare or motion blur are scored only on whether the evidence gate rejects them.

"Needs intervention" is defined from the injected geometry only: a streak at least 150 px long or crossing the part, a short feed patch of at least 500 px, or a super elevation crescent of at least 250 px. The hold-out layers of one build are 40 random heights, not consecutive layers, so trend rules (streaks on 3 of the last 10 layers, super elevation on 2 layers in a row) fire less often than they would on a real build.

Where the agent went wrong

All 13 missed critical layers: the defect was detected in 13 of them (Recoater streak 1, Super elevation 12). The miss is in the policy, not the vision: the label calls a super elevation critical from 250 px of area, the policy stops on a crescent arc of 60 px or on two layers in a row, so crescents under 60 px of arc but over 250 px of area fall between the two; the streak was measured just under the 150 px length rule. 5 of the 5 false stops come from the opposite side of the same rule (a small crescent on two consecutive sampled layers). These policy thresholds were set before the hold-out was generated and are reported unchanged; aligning the label and policy definitions is the first next step.

6. Closed loop demo build

480 layers of the thrust chamber on one simulated machine, run through the same handler the Lambda runs. Actions: CONTINUE 480, RECOAT 4, RE_IMAGE 2, STOP 1, OPERATOR 1. Median handler latency 287 ms (p95 708 ms).

7. Latency

Median 472 ms, p95 794 ms per layer (two frames) on macOS-15.8.1-arm64-arm-64bit, a laptop that was shared with other heavy jobs during the run, so these are upper bounds. Graviton numbers need the AWS deploy (make deploy), which is pending an AWS account.

8. The learned segmenter

U-Net, 482,805 parameters, trained on CPU (4 threads) for 16 epochs of 40 steps (38 minutes wall clock on a shared machine), best validation loss at epoch 13. Exported to ONNX and run with cv.dnn.readNetFromONNX; maximum absolute difference to PyTorch on a random input: 9.5e-06.

9. Limits

  • The headline numbers are on synthetic images whose defects I designed. They show the pipeline does what it is built to do; they do not show real world accuracy. The real image check covers recoat defects only and has box labels.
  • The synthetic camera is 512 x 512 at 0.86 mm per pixel; real machines use more pixels per mm.
  • The demo build samples 480 heights through a 385 mm part, so one demo layer stands for many 40 micron layers.
  • Not a certification tool. A stop is a request for a person to look.
